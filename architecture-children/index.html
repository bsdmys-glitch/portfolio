ACADEMIC THESIS / KINDERGARTEN DESIGN

Architecture and Children

01 / PROJECT RECORD

A kindergarten as a small city for learning, play and community.

Architecture and Children is a thesis project developed at Virginia Tech in May 2019. It asks how early-childhood education can extend beyond the classroom by connecting children, teachers, families and the wider community.

At the scale of the building, classrooms, office, recreation and circulation gather around a central core and atrium. Inside and outside are treated as a continuous learning landscape, with spaces that can change meaning as children move through them.

02 / EDUCATION AS CONTEXT

Learning begins beyond the classroom.

The thesis begins with the history of kindergarten and a comparison of early-childhood education systems. Cognitive, physical, social and emotional development are treated as interconnected conditions rather than separate outcomes.

That research supports a more open idea of the school: a place where children learn through movement, observation, making and contact with the people and environments around them.

PROJECT EVIDENCE / CHILDREN AND SPACE
03 / SCHOOL AS A CITY

Different activities gather around one shared core.

The school is organized like a small city. Office, classroom, recreation and circulation are distinct but connected through a central core and atrium, so the route between rooms becomes part of the learning environment.

The ground and entry levels extend the same structure into a continuous relationship between indoor rooms, outdoor play areas and the surrounding site.

SPATIAL EVIDENCE / CORE AND PROGRAMME
04 / BLURRED BORDER

The kindergarten opens its resources to the community.

The thesis argues that a school building is used for only part of the year. During other hours, the facility could become a community centre, parenting classroom or shared learning resource.

This public role is supported by a spatial sequence that does not isolate children from the outside world. Entrances, gardens, circulation and shared rooms allow the kindergarten to connect everyday education with a wider community.

PUBLIC EVIDENCE / OPEN SCHOOL
05 / DESIGN INTERVENTION

Solid and void create a range of scales for children.

The design develops the idea of heavy and light through intersecting walls, open voids, changing ceiling heights and controlled views. The building gives children spaces for gathering, retreat, observation and movement.

Sections and elevations carry the research into a calm, light-filled proposal: a small building with an internal public life and a strong connection to the landscape of Blacksburg.
