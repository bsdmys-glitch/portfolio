01 / PROJECT RECORD

Continuity is the starting point for renewal.

Jieyang Ancient City is a living river-port city whose historic structure remains embedded in everyday life. The project asked how protection could support continuity and change without turning the city into a museum set.

A research-led framework connected field survey, historical and spatial analysis, protection and renewal guidelines, and street-scale design verification.

02 / RESEARCH BASE

Fieldwork and spatial analysis shaped the renewal framework.

The project combined a 1.5-month field survey of more than 40,000 houses with archival and spatial analysis. Buildings were recorded by use, history and physical condition, while streets, shopfronts, waterways and everyday practices were studied as connected parts of the old city.

The findings were organized across five dimensions: history, society, culture, industry and space. Together, they identified heritage value and the conditions for change, establishing the basis for the protection framework, building-repair guidelines and subsequent street-scale design studies.

03 / SPATIAL FRAMEWORK

The research was organized into an overall framework for protection and renewal.

Field survey records, historical sources and spatial analysis were consolidated into four connected systems: value, protection, planning and implementation. The systems linked the city's heritage buildings, water network, streets, public spaces and everyday activities to decisions about what to retain, what to renew and how to phase change.

At the spatial scale, the framework used the historic water structure, city gates, street network and cultural nodes to organize water restoration, circulation, public life and future uses. At the implementation scale, the overall design was carried forward through the 1314 implementation plan and three guidelines for unit protection, unit renewal and building repair.

  1. 01Value system

    Five dimensions - history, society, culture, industry and space - structured the research findings.

  2. 02Protection system

    Buildings, streets, landscapes, water systems and cultural elements were identified and classified for protection.

  3. 03Planning system

    Water, circulation, public space, culture and industry were coordinated as city-wide strategies.

  4. 04Implementation system

    The 1314 plan and three guidelines connected the framework to phased construction and repair.

04 / GUIDELINES AS DESIGN TOOL

The guideline stage made research usable.

The work did not stop at diagnosis. Historical evidence was translated into rules that could guide decisions from the city unit to the building detail.

Guideline research and compilation connected heritage value with practical repair and renewal decisions.

01Unit protection

Protect historic structure, value and spatial memory.

02Unit renewal

Coordinate public space, movement, use and gradual change.

03Building repair

Address structure, facade, roof, doors, windows, equipment and safety as one system.

05 / DESIGN VERIFICATION

Rules became decisions at street and building scale.

The framework was tested through facade repair, shopfront continuity, public space, commercial exchange and waterfront life. This is where the research became architecture.

01Retain character

Keep arcades, columns, balconies, carved grilles and significant openings legible.

02Support use

Make room for shops, public life and contemporary needs without erasing local identity.

03Coordinate systems

Make signage, services, planting, paving and building repair work as one street condition.
